Chair Sponsorship

You Can Sponsor a Chair

Chair sponsorship gives you "naming rights" for a chair occupied by one of our principal players for the current season. It's an unusual and deeply personal way to support live music.

You can name a chair to memorialize your contribution, celebrate a loved one, give a unique gift, or honor a memory. Chairs are sponsored on a first-come, first-served basis and your full sponsorship is tax deductible.

  • The John & Mary Smith Chair
  • The Hernandez Family Chair
  • The Kim Chiu Memorial Chair
  • The ABC Company Chair
  • The Susan Gray and Deborah Black Chair
  • A chair sponsored by a generous anonymous donor

Available for 2026–27 Season

To reserve a chair, email info@lewisvillesymphony.org. We'll confirm availability, payment method, and the name to display.

Sponsorships & Advertising

Concert & Series Sponsorships

The Symphony welcomes sponsors and co-sponsors for each of the concert series — Symphony, International Chamber, and Pops — and for individual concerts.

Sponsorship includes recognition in printed programs, event signage, and promotional materials. This is meaningful, high-visibility community support.

Commercial sponsors and co-sponsors must be from different business segments. Only four co-sponsors per series or event are accepted. Contact the Symphony for pricing and to confirm availability.

Smart Giving Strategies

Maximize Your Impact

🤝
Double Your Donation with Corporate Matching
Many companies support the arts through grants that match donations made by employees or retirees. Check with your HR or Community Affairs Department — we can provide our non-profit status, EIN number, and any other documentation your company requires.
📈
Turn Capital Gains Into a Tax Deduction
Donate appreciated stock to a charitable fund (such as Schwab or Fidelity) and recommend a grant to the Symphony up to the amount you've donated. This government-blessed strategy lets you turn capital gains into tax deductions. Ask your broker or financial advisor for details.
